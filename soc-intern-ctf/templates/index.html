What the SOC received

Over one shift, alerts fired involving an employee workstation and that employee's corporate account:

  • Suspicious authentication activity
  • PowerShell execution on two hosts
  • Unusual outbound network communication
  • A phishing email reported by an employee
  • Suspicious AWS activity

You are the junior analyst on shift. Decide whether these events are related, and whether a genuine security incident has occurred.

Do not assume every alert is malicious. Some events in this dataset are legitimate administrative or automated activity, and at least one high-severity alert is a false positive. Separating the two is most of the job.

05

Communication

best left to the write-up window

Two short pieces of writing. Both are scored on accuracy and restraint as much as on clarity — do not state as fact anything the evidence has not confirmed.

AIncident summary for the CTO
The CTO has no security background. Cover: what happened, which systems and users may be affected, business impact, your current confidence level, and the immediate action you recommend. Maximum 150 words. Avoid jargon.
BCustomer notification
You are contacting the customer to inform them that suspicious activity has been detected. Professional, factual, and free of any claim that has not been confirmed. Maximum 200 words.

07

Assessment rules

  • The investigation window is 2 hours and starts when you press Start assessment. It does not pause.
  • At 2 hours the dataset locks permanently — search, filters and raw logs close and do not reopen.
  • You then have 30 more minutes to finish your answers from your own notes. At 2 hours 30 minutes everything locks.
  • Refreshing or closing the page does not reset the clock. The deadline is stored, not the time remaining.
  • The written report is due by email within 24 hours of the invitation email you received from Brain Station 23. That clock runs independently of this one and started before you opened this page.
  • External references, documentation and tooling are allowed. Decoding, lookups and note-taking are expected.
  • You may use AI assistance. You must disclose it in section 08 and paste the prompts you used. Prompting is a skill we are interested in; concealment is not a risk worth taking.
  • You must be able to defend every line of your report in a live interview. Answers you cannot explain are treated as unsupported, whoever wrote them.
  • Do not fabricate evidence. An invented IP, hash or log line is an automatic fail.
  • Every conclusion is labelled FACT, HYPOTHESIS, CONFIRMED or UNKNOWN.
  • All data here is fictional. Do not attempt to resolve, scan or contact any domain or address in the dataset.
  • The login page in Challenge 2 is an inert mockup. It does not collect or transmit anything. Never enter a real credential into it.
08

AI use disclosure

required

Using an AI assistant is allowed and is not held against you. Hiding it is. We read this section to understand how you direct a tool, which is a real SOC skill — vague prompting produces confident nonsense, and confident nonsense is the failure mode this whole assessment is built to catch.

Undisclosed AI use discovered during the interview ends the application. Disclosed AI use, with the prompts, costs you nothing.
A1Did you use an AI assistant for any part of this assessment?
A2Which tool or tools, and for which parts?
For example: log triage, decoding, MITRE mapping, drafting the CTO summary, proofreading.
A3Paste the exact prompts you used
Copy them verbatim, including follow-ups. Do not tidy them up — the rough version is more useful to us than a polished one. If you ran many, paste the ones that actually shaped your answer.
A4What did the AI get wrong, and what did you reject?
Name at least one thing you checked and overruled. If the answer is genuinely "nothing", say so and explain how you verified it.
